## Content Strategy: First Account Manager Handoff

Labels: [BRIEF] stated in the brief. [INFERENCE] guess, unverified. No search-demand numbers are used. Check YouTube autocomplete before publishing.

### Best Title
**Build This Before You Hire Your First Account Manager**

Why it wins:
- Angle pattern: "If you want [outcome], build this first." The viewer's existing desire is to get out of day-to-day client work. The title gives them the next step, not a dream.
- Understood in 3 seconds. "First account manager" names the exact viewer. "Build this" promises a real artifact on screen.
- No growth or scale promise, which fits the brief's no-income-claims rule. [BRIEF]

Angle rationale (Offer-Sentence Contract): the title is the first-move version of the offer: "I help owners of 2-8 person marketing or creative agencies hire and onboard their first account manager without a costly bad hire or clients feeling handed off, by building a role scorecard and handoff plan before the hire."

### Hook
First 20 seconds, word for word. Open on the finished scorecard on screen.

"This is the document you build before you hire your first account manager. I help owners of 2-8 person marketing or creative agencies hire and onboard their first account manager without a costly bad hire or clients feeling handed off, by building a role scorecard and handoff plan before the hire. So let's build it, start to finish, right now."

### Retention Map
Target runtime: about 12 minutes. [INFERENCE] Timings are planning guesses.

| Time | Beat | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:20 | Hook above, finished scorecard on screen | Outcome first, offer sentence stated |
| 0:20-1:15 | Reframe: this is a handoff problem, not a recruiting problem [INFERENCE]. Say plainly that the owner has to do work first. | Remove the "hire fixes it with no effort" hope |
| 1:15-1:45 | Roadmap: three documents (scorecard, client handoff map, first-30-days plan). Today we build the scorecard and the handoff map, and show the 30-day plan. | Promise, keep it short |
| 1:45-5:30 | BUILD 1: Role scorecard. What the AM owns, what stays with the owner, what a good first 90 days looks like. Use a clearly labelled example role. | Core real build |
| 5:30-6:15 | Show how to judge a candidate against the written role. No promise a bad hire is impossible. | Bad-hire fear |
| 6:15-9:45 | BUILD 2: Client handoff map for a labelled sample set of clients. For each: who owns what, what the client expects, how you introduce the new person. | "Talk to you, not them" fear |
| 9:45-11:00 | SHOW: First-30-days plan, readable on screen, with check-ins so the owner sees it working or failing early. | Completes the sequence |
| 11:00-11:30 | Honest limits: this does not decide whether you can afford the hire. The scorecard makes the scope clear so you can judge cost. | Trust, objection handling |
| 11:30-12:00 | CTA: free scorecard and handoff checklist by email. Coaching program mentioned once, as the step after the template. | YouTube to email path |

### Screen Recording Beats
1. 0:00 Full-frame cut to the finished scorecard. Hold 3 seconds before speaking.
2. 0:20 Cut to a blank scorecard. Show the empty sections so the build is visible.
3. 1:45 Type the role title. Zoom to the "owns" column and fill it live.
4. 3:00 Zoom to the "stays with the owner" column. This is the letting-go step. Pause on it.
5. 4:30 Zoom to the 90-day expectations. Show the finished scorecard side by side with the blank start.
6. 5:30 Zoom on the candidate criteria rows. Show one row used as a pass/fail check.
7. 6:15 Switch to the handoff map. Label the sample clients "EXAMPLE" on screen.
8. 7:30 Zoom on one client row: the expectation field and the introduction step. Show the intro wording typed out.
9. 9:45 Cut to the 30-day plan. Zoom on the week-1 and week-2 check-in rows. Hold long enough to read.
10. 11:00 Cut to a plain slide or document for the affordability caveat. No numbers unless sourced.
11. 11:30 Show the template download page and email form. Cut at the end of the CTA.

Cut anything that is theory or general agency advice. Every minute should show a document being filled in.

### Production notes
- Personal story: say only what is real. The brief marks the coach identity as "example only," so confirm the real background before filming. [BRIEF]
- No income claims, client results or testimonials in the title, thumbnail, or script. [BRIEF]
- No AI angle is forced; the build is a process and a set of documents.
