## Offer Architecture: First Account Manager Handoff

Evidence labels: [BRIEF] = stated in the business brief. [INFERENCE] = reasoned guess from the market brief, not verified. [HYPOTHESIS] = my offer or price guess, to be tested. No live research was done, so there are no [PUBLIC] claims.

### One-Sentence Offer

I help owners of 2-8 person marketing or creative agencies hire and onboard their first account manager without a costly bad hire or clients feeling handed off, by building a role scorecard and handoff plan before the hire.

### Positioning
- For: [BRIEF] owners of small marketing or creative agencies (about 2-8 people) who handle every client relationship themselves and have never hired an account manager.
- Position: the handoff-first way to hire an account manager. Define the role, the client handoff and the owner's own letting go before recruiting.
- Reframe: [INFERENCE] owners likely see this as a recruiting problem; it is more likely a handoff problem. The video leads with that reframe.
- Not positioned as: growth, scaling or income promises. [BRIEF] No income claims.

### Core Offer
- Urgent problem: [INFERENCE] The owner can't take on new clients or take time off without client relationships suffering. Hiring looks like the fix, but they don't know how to do it.
- Measurable outcome: [BRIEF] a hired, onboarded first account manager. Checkable deliverables: a completed role scorecard, a client-by-client handoff list, and a written first-30-days plan.
- Format: [BRIEF] a short coaching program. Length, cohort vs 1:1 and price are not in the brief. [HYPOTHESIS] 4-6 weeks, 1:1 with templates, about $1,500-$3,000. Test with real buyers before using.
- Why now: [INFERENCE] Owner capacity is a daily constraint, and a mis-hire costs a large share of a small team's budget. Waiting means the owner stays the bottleneck.
- Next step after the video: get the free role scorecard and handoff checklist by email. The coaching program is offered after that.

### Unique Mechanism
"Handoff before hire": a three-document sequence. No AI angle is forced; the system is a process and a set of documents.
1. Role scorecard. What the account manager owns, what stays with the owner, and what a good first 90 days looks like.
2. Client handoff map. For each client, who owns what, what the client expects, and how the owner introduces the new person.
3. First-30-days plan. Onboarding steps and check-ins so the owner can see it working or failing early.

Why it is believable: the video builds one of these documents on screen. [BRIEF] The coach has run a small agency and hired account managers. Credibility rests on the process shown, not on outcomes claimed. [INFERENCE] The risk is that it sounds like generic "delegate and scale" advice, so the artifact has to be shown, not described.

### Proof Requirements
Shown on screen. All of it must be real. Nothing is invented.
- The actual scorecard, built live in the video, with a real or clearly labelled example role.
- The handoff map filled in for a sample set of clients, labelled as an example.
- The first-30-days plan on screen, readable.
- The coach's real background, stated as it is. [BRIEF] The identity is "example only," so any personal story must be real before filming.
- No income claims, no client results, no testimonials. [BRIEF] If real testimonials exist later, add them then.
- Any search or demand statistic: shown with its source, or left out. [INFERENCE] Demand is unverified. Check YouTube autocomplete and Google before publishing.

### Objection Map
| Objection | How the video addresses it |
|---|---|
| "I'm afraid of a bad first hire." [INFERENCE] | Show the scorecard criteria so the owner can judge candidates against a written role. No promise that a bad hire is impossible. |
| "Clients will say they want to talk to me, not them." [INFERENCE] | Show the client handoff map and the introduction step, handled one client at a time. |
| "I can't afford the hire." [INFERENCE] | Do not dodge it. Say the video does not decide affordability. The scorecard makes the role's scope explicit so the owner can judge the cost. No numbers unless sourced. |
| "I'll hand the burden off with little effort." [INFERENCE] | Say directly that the owner has to do work first: define the role and hand over clients on purpose. |
| "This is generic scale-your-agency advice." [INFERENCE] | Open with the finished document on screen, not with a promise. |
| "Why pay for coaching when there's a free template?" | The template is the first step. The program covers applying it to this owner's clients, hiring and onboarding. |
| "Does this person know what they're talking about?" | Show the process. State only the real background. No outcome claims. |
