### Lead Magnet

**Name:** Former-Peer Conversation Runbook [PLACEHOLDER: coach confirms the name; this is the working label from 02 and 03].

**Format:** one email-gated download, three parts, all usable the same day. Not a generic PDF: the sections, field names and order are the ones the viewer watched being filled in.

**Promise:**

I help first-time engineering managers hold their first underperformance conversation with a former peer within a week, without avoiding it or souring the relationship, by building a rehearsed, reviewed conversation runbook.

Honest scope line for the opt-in page, directly under the sentence above [DECISION]: "This download is the first half of that runbook, the half you can build alone tonight. The rehearsed and reviewed half needs a second person, and it is optional." This keeps the free page truthful: the template alone does not rehearse or review anything. It also does not claim the person improves, stays or stays friendly [OFFER].

**Contents (what is in the download):**

1. **The blank one-page runbook**, six numbered sections, matching the page shown at 0:00 and 11:15 in the video [CONTENT]:
   - Header: scenario line, with a "Hypothetical" label on the example copy only. Anonymization format printed in the header (no names, no company, generic role) [OFFER Objection 7].
   - 1 Reproduce: facts box (date, deliverable, review comment per row), plus a "labels to cross out" strip for words like "unmotivated".
   - 2 Define expected behavior: one-sentence bar, with the "same for any report" checkbox and the "would I say this to a stranger on the team?" prompt.
   - 3 Write the opening: first 30 seconds word for word, a field for the line that names the former-peer relationship once, and a weak-opening versus revised-opening split.
   - 4 Test the failure cases: three-row table (likely reply, your response), pre-seeded with the three the video uses ("you did this too", "nobody told me", silence), plus the support-offered field and a follow-up date field.
   - 5 Rehearse: an instruction box and a "where the script broke" margin note ("Rehearse this."). It tells the reader to say the opening out loud to another person who will push back. It does not teach more than the video does.
   - 6 Ship and monitor: recap-note lines and a check-against-observable-facts line. Same depth as the video: named, not taught on the reader's own case [CONTENT Design note].
2. **The filled example**: the same hypothetical runbook shown on screen, so the viewer can compare their draft to a finished one. Labeled "Hypothetical, not a client" on every page. [PLACEHOLDER: coach writes the scenario text; 03 asks for a plausible, generic case such as review comments that keep reopening the same issue and deliverables that slip.]
3. **A 10-point readiness checklist** [PLACEHOLDER: coach writes or confirms; 02 and 03 both mark this content as the coach's]. Draft for the coach to edit, derived only from the six steps [DECISION]:
   1. Every fact in section 1 is observable (date, deliverable, comment), with no labels.
   2. The bar in section 2 is one sentence and would be the same for any report.
   3. I would say that bar to a stranger on the team.
   4. The first 30 seconds are written word for word.
   5. The opening names the former-peer relationship once, on purpose.
   6. I have a written response to each of three likely replies.
   7. I know what support I am offering.
   8. A follow-up date is set.
   9. This case is not a legal, protected-class or safety issue. If it is, go to HR first.
   10. I have said the opening out loud to another person who pushed back. (Most people leave this one unchecked. That is the gap the video points at.)

**Why someone wants it now:**
- They just watched the build and have a real person in mind. The viewer is in the exact situation the title names ("Your Old Teammate Now Reports to You"), and the fastest way to use the video is to fill in their own copy tonight.
- The video stands alone for steps 1 to 4, so the template is a convenience and a finished example, not a ransom. That is why it is safe to trust: they can test the method before spending anything, which is the stated answer to the coach's weak credibility [OFFER Objection 2; MARKET credibility 1 of 5 per 03].
- Delay makes the situation heavier each week [INFERENCE, no statistic to be cited].

### Follow-Up Sequence

Sender: the coach by name [PLACEHOLDER: coach name and real-person sending address]. Plain text, no images, short. Voice: direct, practical, engineer-friendly. Reply-to is monitored by the coach, because Email 1 asks for replies. Every email carries the unsubscribe link and mailing address [PLACEHOLDER]. No testimonials, results or fake scarcity in any email. The premium program is not mentioned in this sequence [OFFER: test the entry step first].

Timing is [HYPOTHESIS]: Day 0, 2, 4, 6, 9.

### Sales Path

The ladder is the one in 02, in order, with one new step per touch [OFFER]:

1. **Video** (free): builds steps 1 to 4 on a hypothetical. Stands alone. Ends at the CTA above.
2. **Lead magnet** (free, email): blank runbook, filled example, checklist. The reader proves the method to themselves. Item 10 of the checklist is the deliberate pointer to step 5.
3. **Emails 1 to 5** (free): finish the free half (Emails 1 and 2), explain the gap honestly (Email 3), remove objections (Email 4), close the loop (Email 5).
4. **Entry paid step: Review and Rehearse** [PLACEHOLDER: name], one session on the reader's own anonymized case, runbook returned afterward. Price is [HYPOTHESIS: 02 suggests testing $150 to $400; untested, set only after 5 to 10 conversations with target managers]. Enter via the booking link in Email 1's P.S., Email 3 and Email 5, plus replies to the "dreaded pushback" question. A reply to Email 1 is the warmest lead: answer it personally, and let the reply shape the session intake.
5. **Premium multi-week 1:1** [PLACEHOLDER: name; price hypothesis in 02 is untested]. It is offered only inside or after a completed session, by the coach's judgment, and only once the entry step shows people buy and finish [OFFER]. It is not in the video, not in the opt-in page and not in this email sequence.
6. **Employer package**: separate path, separate video, separate proof. Email 4 only gives an individual the reimbursement paragraph. Do not merge the buyers [OFFER].

Why this feels like one system: the thing sold at step 4 is the part of the offer sentence ("rehearsed, reviewed") that the free steps cannot deliver. The viewer experienced the gap on the video (the script breaks in the role-play), felt it in checklist item 10, and heard it named again in Email 3.

What to measure first [HYPOTHESIS, no targets claimed]: video-to-opt-in rate; Email 1 open and reply rate; the pushback replies as qualitative market research; booking-link clicks per email; session bookings; and how many sessions end with the conversation held within 7 days (the offer's process outcome) [OFFER]. If bookings are zero but replies are many, the price or the unproven-coach doubt is the problem, not the content.

Before launch: the coach completes the blockers in 02 (name and background, experience or "none yet", pricing, refund policy, booking system, scope statement) [OFFER Blockers 1, 2, 5, 6]. Without them the sequence still sends, but Emails 3 and 4 have gaps.

### Friction Check

Things that would make the path confusing or forced, with the fix:

1. **The free download does not deliver the full sentence.** The offer sentence describes a rehearsed, reviewed runbook; the template only covers the part built alone. Fix: the scope line on the opt-in page and in Email 1 says so plainly. Do not let the page imply that the file alone achieves the sentence.
2. **The template must match the video exactly.** If the six numbered sections, the field names, the "same for any report" checkbox, the three-row table or the follow-up date field differ from what is on screen, the CTA ("the blank one-page runbook... the filled example you just watched") becomes untrue. Fix: build the template from the screen recording, not from this file, and keep the filled example identical to the on-screen hypothetical.
3. **The video promises "a link in the email".** Email 1 must contain the session link. If the booking system is not ready [PLACEHOLDER: 02 Blocker 5], either delay the video or change the CTA line to remove that promise. A dead link in the first email is the worst break in the path.
4. **No price or program name in the video, but a price in the email.** That is intended, but the first price the viewer sees must not feel like a trap. Email 3 states what it is and is not, plus the refund or no-fit policy [PLACEHOLDER]. If the coach has no policy, the pilot framing must be stated honestly.
5. **Credibility gap.** The weakest link [OFFER; MARKET credibility 1 of 5]. The 0:22-1:15 beat and Email 3 each need a truthful statement or "none yet". If the coach cannot supply one, remove the "[PLACEHOLDER: coach's experience]" lines rather than fill them with vague authority language.
6. **The checklist is not in the video.** It is a bonus in the download, not a second CTA. The coach should not mention it on camera in the same breath as the template.
7. **Checklist item 10 is deliberately a pointer to the paid step.** It must stay honest and not read as a trick. If the coach thinks it feels salesy, move the line to Email 3 only.
8. **Email 1 asks a question and gives a P.S. link.** Two asks in one email. The reply request is primary; the P.S. is there only because the video promised it. Do not add a third.
9. **Replies need a human.** If the sequence asks for replies and nobody answers, the path loses its best data and the feeling of help. Fix: the coach commits to reading and answering replies personally [PLACEHOLDER].
10. **Privacy.** Anonymization instructions are in the header, Email 1 and Email 3. A reader who pastes real names into the reply or into an LLM is a risk. If the LLM tip stays in the video, carry the warning into the template.
11. **Sentence stiffness.** The offer sentence is long and, as 03 flags, can sound stiff aloud. Captions, the opt-in page and Email 1 must keep the exact words. Spoken delivery may pause, not change.
12. **Email compliance and consent** [PLACEHOLDER: the coach's tool, consent wording, mailing address, jurisdiction rules].
13. **Demand is not validated** [OFFER Blocker 7]. Open rates, reply rates and price points are guesses until the coach runs 5 to 10 target-manager conversations and gets real search data.
14. **Day-9 "gets heavier" line.** It is [INFERENCE]. If the coach has no experience to stand behind it, cut the sentence from Email 5.
15. **Attribution.** Situation-Behavior-Impact is credited in the video. If the template uses its field names, the credit must appear on the template as well, after the attribution check [OFFER Blocker 8].
