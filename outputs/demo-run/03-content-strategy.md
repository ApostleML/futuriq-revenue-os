## Content Strategy: First-time engineering manager coaching (demo-run)

Evidence labels: [BRIEF], [MARKET] (01-market-signal.md), [OFFER] (02-offer-architecture.md), [DECISION] my choice, [HYPOTHESIS] untested, [PLACEHOLDER: ...] the coach must supply. No credentials, results, testimonials or view-count claims are made here. Titles and thumbnails are untested; the market file says clickability is provisional until real search and competitor data exists [MARKET].

Adaptation note [DECISION]: this is not an AI-tool video. The "real build" is filling in the Conversation Runbook, live on screen, on a hypothetical scenario that is labeled as hypothetical on screen the whole time. No AI angle is used.

### Offer Sentence (quoted from 02-offer-architecture.md, "One-Sentence Offer")

I help first-time engineering managers hold their first underperformance conversation with a former peer within a week, without avoiding it or souring the relationship, by building a rehearsed, reviewed conversation runbook.

### Angle

Pattern used: "I built [specific system] so you can copy it."

Rationale: the offer is this sentence, quoted exactly: "I help first-time engineering managers hold their first underperformance conversation with a former peer within a week, without avoiding it or souring the relationship, by building a rehearsed, reviewed conversation runbook." The video builds the free half of that runbook on screen (steps 1 to 4 of 6) so the viewer can copy it tonight. The paid half, the rehearsal and review of their own case, is the part a template cannot do [OFFER]. The "build" is the runbook, and the engineer-friendly framing (reproduce, define expected behavior, test failure cases) is the reason a skeptical viewer keeps watching [MARKET, INFERENCE].

Honest-claims rule for the whole video: say "the process I use" and "run it on your own situation and judge it". Never say "proven", never imply client results. The offer promises a process outcome (the conversation is held, expectations are written down, a follow-up date is set), not that the person improves or stays [OFFER].

### Best Title

**Your Old Teammate Now Reports to You. Do This First.**

Why it wins:
- Understood in 3 seconds. It names the exact situation and the viewer is the "you". No tool name, no theory.
- It matches the viewer's existing state, not a desired abstraction. Engineers likely search by situation ("managing former peers") more than by "leadership coaching" [MARKET, INFERENCE].
- It sidesteps the crowded "first 90 days" frame [MARKET, PUBLIC].
- It makes no result claim, so it stays inside the brief's constraints. "Do this first" implies a build, which the video delivers.
- Hypothesis: test against the backup titles before publishing. Do not treat it as proven.

### Hook

First 20 seconds, word for word (about 22 seconds at a calm pace; trim the pause if it runs long). Cold open, no logo, no intro. On screen throughout: a blank one-page runbook titled "Hypothetical scenario, not a client".

> "Your old teammate now reports to you. They're missing the bar. And you've been putting off the conversation. I help first-time engineering managers hold their first underperformance conversation with a former peer within a week, without avoiding it or souring the relationship, by building a rehearsed, reviewed conversation runbook. So let's build the first half of that runbook, live, right now."

Timing check: line 1 about 5 seconds, the offer sentence about 13 seconds, the bridge about 3 seconds. The offer sentence is unaltered and unbroken inside the quote; only the delivery pauses naturally.

Hook checks:
- It opens on the viewer's situation and desired outcome, not on a tool or a method.
- It does not claim any credential. "I help" is the offer sentence as written. The credibility moment comes in the next section, as the offer file's Objection 2 requires.
- Production flag: the sentence is long. If it sounds stiff when spoken, the coach may split the delivery with pauses but must not change the words on any lower-third or caption.

Cut list (filler to remove): any definition of leadership, any "why management is hard" montage, any list of "5 mistakes" beyond the one scenario, any employer or L&D pitch, any mention of the premium program or its price, any story about a client unless the coach has real, consented material [PLACEHOLDER].

Design note: steps 5 and 6 (rehearse, ship and monitor) are mentioned but not taught on the viewer's own case. That is the line between free and paid [OFFER]. The video must still stand alone: the viewer should be able to complete a usable draft of steps 1 to 4 after watching.

### Screen Recording Beats

Everything shown is a hypothetical. Keep a persistent small label "Hypothetical scenario, not a client" in the corner of every runbook shot.

1. 0:00 Show the blank runbook page at full screen with six numbered sections visible. Hold for the hook.
2. 0:08 Zoom to the scenario title line as the viewer-situation sentence is spoken (lower-third: "Hypothetical").
3. 0:09-0:22 Do not cut away during the offer sentence. Keep the coach on camera or the blank runbook on screen. If a caption is shown, it must match the quoted sentence exactly.
4. 0:22 Cut to the coach on camera for the credibility section. On screen, show only what the coach can truthfully support [PLACEHOLDER]. No logos, badges or numbers that are not real.
5. 1:30 Show the Purdue and BYU study pages as cited links, zoom on the sample sizes (16 and 220), and add a caption "small samples". Do not paraphrase beyond what the abstracts support [MARKET].
6. 2:00 Show the scenario text typed into the runbook. Zoom on the "Hypothetical" label.
7. 2:30 Step 1: type observable facts into the box, then show the label "unmotivated" being struck through and replaced. Zoom on the strikethrough.
8. 3:30 Show the Situation-Behavior-Impact source credit on screen (pending the attribution check) beside the runbook field names.
9. 4:00 Step 2: type the one-sentence bar. Zoom on the sentence. Show the "same for any report" test as a small checkbox.
10. 5:15 Step 3: type the opening line word for word, visible at the lowest third. Do a split-screen: weak opening versus revised opening. Zoom on the line that names the former-peer relationship.
11. 6:30 Cut to the coach on camera speaking both openings, so the viewer hears the difference.
12. 7:30 Step 4: show the three pushbacks as a table (reply, response). Fill in the first live; time-cut the other two.
13. 8:45 Show the completed support and follow-up date fields. Highlight the follow-up date in a visible color.
14. 9:15 Cut to a two-chair or split role-play shot with a persistent label "Demonstration, not a real case". Show the script alongside only for the opening and pushbacks, then drop it when the coach goes off-script.
15. 10:20 Freeze on the moment the script breaks. Zoom on that line in the runbook and add a margin note: "Rehearse this."
16. 10:30 Show a book list graphic (Watkins, Stanier, Drasner) for two seconds, titled "Good for principles". Then show the single runbook line for this case beside the general principle. This is the visual gap [OFFER Objection 1].
17. 11:15 Show the template download link and the filled runbook next to the blank template. One link, one URL. No price on screen.
